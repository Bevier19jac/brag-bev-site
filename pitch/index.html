Rubric: Market Opportunity. There are about 118 million homes in this country, and a lot of them roll out two bins every week, trash and recycling. Every one of those bins gets grimy and starts to smell. Today the options are a hose and a scrub brush, or paying a service twenty-plus dollars a visit, so most people just live with it. Dumpster Diver is single-use, one pod per bin, every week. Here in Fayette County alone that's more than 46,000 homes. My first customer is the homeowner. After that: HOAs, property managers and the bin-cleaning companies themselves.

Rubric: proof the solution works. I didn't want to just tell you it works, so I paid an independent lab to test it. ARE Labs dropped it from 36 inches into a standard 96-gallon bin, three times. It fired all three times, and foam landed on all sixteen test points, from the bottom of the wall all the way up to the underside of the lid. The lab also flagged that the earlier shell tipped over after firing. That's exactly what we fixed next.

Brag & Bev · Dumpster Diver12
Challenges and support. Here's what stands in my way, and all of it is solvable. The prototype works, but it's still a prototype. It's heavier than it needs to be. The actuator is glued on, and that's exactly why Trial 4 didn't fire. I want a custom nozzle for a better spray, and a short delay so it fires after the lid is closed. Our test bin is a cheap store bin with a steep beveled bottom that tips the pod into the wall. A standard hauler bin, like my AM Waste bin, is flat and much bigger, and the production base will settle in either one. Then I need a real per-unit cost, and so far I've paid for every bit of this myself. What I'm asking for: a north star, someone who has taken a physical product from prototype to a store shelf. A production partner. Pilot partners here in Fayette. And capital aimed at those steps.
